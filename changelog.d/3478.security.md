The command-policy deterministic risk scan now flags **cwd/whole-tree
destructive deletes** as `destructive` (→ deny), closing a hole where a prompt
injection could wipe the working directory without ever naming `/`. Covered
shapes, all flag-order-insensitive and matched even when wrapped
(`sh -c …`, `powershell -c "…"`, `cmd /c "…"`):

- **UNIX:** `rm -rf .` / `rm -rf ./*` / `rm -rf *` and the
  `find . -delete` / `find . -exec rm …` family.
- **Windows cmd.exe:** `rmdir`/`rd`/`del`/`erase` with `/s` targeting the cwd or
  a glob (`rd /s /q .`, `del /f /s /q *`), any drive-root delete
  (`del c:\*.*`, `rd /s /q c:\`), and `format <drive>`.
- **PowerShell:** `Remove-Item` and its aliases (`ri`/`rm`/`del`/`rmdir`/`rd`/
  `erase`) with `-Recurse` (incl. abbreviations `-r`/`-rec`) targeting
  `.`/`./*`/`*`/`$pwd`/a drive root, honoring `-Path`/`-LiteralPath`. `-Force` is
  irrelevant to the judgment — recursion alone wipes.

Deliberate boundary preserved: a recursive delete of a *named* subdirectory
(`rm -rf build/`, `rmdir /s /q build`, `Remove-Item -Recurse src\`) is a normal
clean and is not flagged.
